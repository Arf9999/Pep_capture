OSINT Discovery Methodology & Research Limitations

A transparent explanation of the automated discovery pipeline, confidence scoring matrix, deep profile inspection heuristics, and critical research limitations for Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) in South Africa.

🔍 The Discovery & Verification Process

The pipeline automates open-source intelligence (OSINT) discovery for South African Local Government Election candidates by searching across primary social networks (LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, and X/Twitter) alongside creator landing pages and link-in-bio hubs (Linktree, Beacons, Carrd, Taplink, Pallyy, Lnk.Bio, and Bio Sites by Squarespace), resolving identity variations, and scoring match confidence.

1

Candidate Ingestion & Naming Normalization

Candidate records from the official Electoral Commission (IEC) dataset are ingested with their unique identifier (e.g. pers_18589), formal registered name, party affiliation, and electoral district/ward.

2

Multi-Permutation Name Resolution

Social media profiles rarely match official ballot papers exactly. The pipeline generates all realistic cultural naming permutations: First Last, Last First (surname-first registrations), Middle Last (candidates using English/middle names), Last Middle, First Middle (profiles omitting legal surnames on Facebook), and full 3-name variants.

3

Geographic & Municipal Cluster Expansion

Because personal profiles often mention suburban areas or main towns rather than the formal municipal code, district identifiers are expanded into known municipal clusters (e.g. DC21 - Ugu expands to Port Shepstone, Margate, Hibiscus Coast, KwaZulu-Natal; NW403 - Matlosana expands to Klerksdorp, Jouberton, Kanana, Stilfontein).

4

Two-Stage Confidence Evaluation & Link-in-Bio Extraction

Stage 1 (Preview Evaluation): Google South Africa (gl=za) organic search results are evaluated against naming tokens, granular location mentions, and campaign keywords.
Stage 2 (Deep Profile & Link-in-Bio Inspection): For any profile scoring ≥ 0.35, the system performs live inspection of the public profile page. For creator hubs (Linktree, Beacons, Carrd, Taplink, Lnk.Bio, Bio.site, Pallyy), the crawler automatically parses outward links to surface connected WhatsApp numbers, Facebook pages, Twitter handles, and campaign portals.

5

Exclusion Rules & Popolo Export

The system applies strict disqualification rules against official political party organizational accounts (e.g. DA Official, ANC Regional Office) and directory aggregator pages (/public/, /pub/dir/). Discovered accounts are stored in the Popolo Civic Data standard format in both SQLite and JSON.

⚖️ Disciplined Confidence Scoring Matrix

To prevent false positives from common South African names and broad geographic references, the scoring engine enforces strict multi-tier weighting. Basic name similarity alone receives low baseline confidence, broad country-level confirmation provides zero score boost, and failing to match South Africa incurs negative scoring.

Evaluation Component Weight / Score Criteria & Disciplined OSINT Rationale
Full 3-Name Exact Match +0.30 Exact First + Middle + Last name alignment (distinctive individual identity).
First + Last Name Match +0.18 Basic name match. Intentionally conservative because similar names are highly prevalent.
Middle + Last Name Match +0.15 Candidate using registered middle name and surname on professional profile.
Granular Town / Suburb Match +0.30 Specific local town or suburb (e.g. Margate, Port Shepstone, Klerksdorp, Jouberton, Mdantsane).
District / Municipality Match +0.20 Formal district municipality or metro (e.g. Ugu, Matlosana, City of Johannesburg, Buffalo City).
Province Match +0.05 Broad regional province alignment (e.g. KwaZulu-Natal, North West, Eastern Cape, Gauteng).
Broad Country ('South Africa' / 'ZA') +0.00 Country baseline only. Does not grant confidence boost without local granularity.
Political Party Affiliation +0.30 Explicit match with candidate's registered political party (e.g. ANC, DA, EFF, ActionSA, IFP, PA, VF Plus).
Civic / Councillor Role Match +0.25 Explicit civic roles: councillor, ward councillor, mayoral candidate, PR councillor, municipal council, IEC.
Direct Profile URL Structure +0.05 Verified personal profile page URL (excluding directory listings, hashtag pages, and video feeds).
Non-Matching South Africa Penalty -0.25 Profile lacks any South African municipal, provincial, or national geographic anchors.
Foreign Location Penalty -0.50 Profile explicitly located in a foreign country or international city (UK, USA, Nigeria, Kenya, India, etc.).
Conflicting Party Claim Penalty -0.50 Profile explicitly claims public office or active leadership in an opposing rival party.
Party Apparatus / Org Penalty -0.50 Identified as an official branch, regional office, or media team rather than an individual.
Strict Operational Confidence Tiers:
• HIGH (≥ 0.70): Multi-factor verified candidate. Requires full name + granular local town/suburb + confirmed political party or councillor role.
• MEDIUM (0.40 – 0.69): Probable match with exact name and specific local municipality/town or civic context. Warranted for secondary deep inspection.
• LOW (0.20 – 0.39): Tentative match. Name matched with generic country anchor only; unverified without local or party corroboration.
• DISQUALIFIED (< 0.20): Discarded as false positive or foreign account.

⚠️ Critical Research Limitations & Caveats

Users and investigative researchers must evaluate discovered profiles in light of the inherent structural constraints of automated OSINT collection:

🔒 1. Private & Friends-Only Accounts

A substantial proportion of local candidates configure their Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok profiles with strict privacy settings (*friends-only* or *mutuals*). These accounts are completely invisible to search spiders and unauthenticated crawlers, meaning genuine candidate profiles may not be discoverable without direct follower authorization.

🤖 2. Pages Unindexed by Search Robots

Candidate accounts created recently for campaign purposes, accounts with noindex directives, or platforms with restricted search indexing (such as Telegram channels or closed groups) do not appear in Google search indices. The pipeline can only discover profiles that search engine robots have indexed.

🧱 3. Platform Restrictions & Walled Gardens

Major social networks (especially LinkedIn, Facebook, and Instagram) aggressively enforce authentication firewalls, login interstitials, and session limits for automated inspection. While preview snippets and OpenGraph metadata provide surface corroboration, full timelines and private group memberships remain inaccessible behind walled gardens.

🛡️ 4. CAPTCHAs & Bot Mitigation Systems

Automated secondary fetching can encounter bot mitigation challenges (such as Cloudflare Turnstile, Google reCAPTCHA, or DataDome). Automated scrapers that encounter these defenses cannot solve them autonomously, requiring investigators to perform manual in-browser verification using authenticated accounts.

🧠 5. LLM & Heuristic Scoring Constraints

Algorithmic scoring models evaluate available textual strings. Profiles with minimal biographical text, figurative quotes, or sarcastic political commentary may evade automated scoring. Conversely, a profile that mentions a political party in passing (e.g. sharing news) can be misinterpreted if not carefully contextualized by human review.

👥 6. Highly Prevalent Names in Specific Regions

In South Africa, certain surnames, patronymics, and clan names (e.g. Dlamini, Khumalo, Ndlovu, Mkhize, Sithole, Botha, Van der Merwe) are shared by thousands of residents in the same municipal district. When candidates possess common first names without unique middle names or documented party bios, automated tools may identify another resident with the identical name. Always cross-reference with municipal council lists before publication.

📋 Best Practices for Investigators & Journalists

When using this dataset for investigative reporting, public accountability, or civic tech applications:

  • Verify High-Impact Findings Manually: Never publish an attribution relying solely on automated heuristic scores. Check profile photos against campaign posters and official IEC ballot photos.
  • Inspect the Popolo Specification: Review the raw Popolo JSON drawer for each candidate to examine the exact reasoning, triggered signal flags, and date of discovery.
  • Cross-Check Political Affiliations: Verify that recent social media activity is consistent with the candidate's declared political party for the 2026 local government elections.
  • Preserve Evidence: Social media posts and accounts may be deleted or modified during election cycles. Investigators should archive relevant profile snapshots using tools like the Wayback Machine or Archive.today.
